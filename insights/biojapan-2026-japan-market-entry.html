BioJapan 2026 Recap:
Reflections on Japan Market Entry

What three days of conversations revealed about the research, content, and relationship-building work international life science teams need in Japan.

Akiko Yoshida, MSc · October 12, 2026

I attended BioJapan 2026 in Minato Mirai, Yokohama!

Although I have been involved in biotechnology for around 15 years, including my time as a student, this was my first BioJapan.

Alongside partnering meetings representing Pauling.AI, I received meeting requests through LinkedIn and my own network. My schedule quickly filled up across all three days, with conversations spanning companies, university researchers, and support organizations.

The event gave me an opportunity to reflect on the support international life science companies need in Japan—and how my own experience can contribute.

“Entering Japan” means different things to different companies

Without sharing details of individual conversations, the topics ranged from finding customers and research collaborators to identifying partners and developing Japanese-language content.

Even when companies share the same goal of entering Japan, their immediate needs can be very different. Some want to assess the market opportunity. Others are looking for specific organizations to approach, while some need to build awareness of an existing product or service.

Several conversations also touched on the challenges of entering the Japanese market. They reinforced the importance of first understanding where a company is facing difficulties and what it needs to learn before moving forward.

From interest in a technology to a concrete discussion

Another takeaway was the work required to turn interest in a technology into a meaningful discussion about adoption or collaboration.

In conversations with pharmaceutical companies, technical capabilities were only part of the picture. Understanding the other party’s research priorities, existing resources, and potential use cases was just as important.

These discussions reminded me that relationship building takes time. Answering questions, providing relevant information, and following up thoughtfully can help create the conditions for the next conversation.

Supporting that work is an important part of helping companies explore opportunities in Japan.

Supporting your team on a fractional basis

The event also helped me clarify how I would like to work with international life science companies: as an external research and strategy support member, contributing to defined tasks on a fractional basis.

Depending on your needs, I can support three areas.

01 · Research

Scientific, technology, and competitive research

I review scientific literature, technology trends, and competing approaches, organizing findings into comparison tables or reports tailored to the project’s objectives.

Support can also include Japan market research, key opinion leader (KOL) mapping, and the identification and prioritization of potential research or business partners.

This can help address questions such as:

  • How do competing technologies compare?
  • Which researchers or organizations in Japan are working in a relevant field?
  • Which potential partners should we investigate further?
02 · Content

Japanese SEO and content localization

I support Japanese SEO article and blog planning, content writing, English-to-Japanese content localization, and the review and improvement of existing Japanese content.

The aim is to preserve scientific accuracy while making information accessible and relevant to readers in Japan.

I work with international life science companies as well as marketing agencies serving life science clients.

03 · Outreach

Targeted outreach and meeting coordination

Once the target audience and objectives are defined, I can support outreach, initial correspondence, and meeting coordination.

Some companies may benefit from strengthening their Japanese-language content first. Others may need to identify a small number of relevant organizations and begin direct conversations.

The starting point depends on the company’s priorities, with responsibilities and scope agreed in advance.

My Background

A foundation in life science research

Life science research is the foundation of my work. My experience includes scientific literature reviews, technology landscape research, and competitive technology analysis across R&D-related topics.

At Genomelink, I also worked on the company’s own blog content and SEO. Through a marketing agency, I have supported Japanese SEO and localization projects for a major life science company. I have since been approached again about a new biotech-related project.

These experiences connect two parts of my work: researching and understanding scientific information, and communicating it clearly to an intended audience.

I also continue to receive repeat requests for mentoring, helping people organize their ideas, clarify challenges, and consider their next steps.

You may need a competitive technology review, ongoing Japanese content support, or help identifying relevant organizations and coordinating introductory meetings.

These are all starting points for a conversation.

BioJapan gave me a clearer view of the different challenges companies face—and the practical work needed to support their next steps in Japan.

Thank you to everyone who took the time to meet with me over the three days. I look forward to staying in touch.
