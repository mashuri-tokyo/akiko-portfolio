Drug Discovery Platforms and Small-Molecule Drug Discovery

1. PeptiDream

PeptiDream says it uses its proprietary Peptide Discovery Platform System (PDPS) to advance the research and development of medicines based on macrocyclic peptides, both in-house and with partner companies.

According to the company, PDPS can create libraries containing more than 10 trillion macrocyclic peptides. The platform uses a cell-free transcription/translation system with a random DNA library and can incorporate non-natural amino acids as well as natural amino acids. Each peptide is barcoded with a corresponding mRNA/cDNA tag, allowing its sequence to be identified rapidly.

The company says its screening process includes selection against the intended biological target and counter-screening against off-targets to identify hit peptides with high binding affinity and selectivity. The barcode allows repeated selection cycles to amplify hit peptides and identify their sequences. PeptiDream reports that it can identify hit compounds in about one month, with a success rate of more than 95% for hit identification.

The company also says PDPS can be used for peptide optimization and hit evaluation. It continues to improve the platform by expanding the range of amino acids, developing automated processes, and building in silico analysis systems.

Official information: PeptiDream: Technology · PDPS page (linked from the page above)

2. Chordia Therapeutics

Chordia Therapeutics is a startup developing next-generation anticancer drugs based on scientific evidence. The company focuses on RNA regulatory abnormalities in cancer and says its small-molecule pipeline is based on a concept of inducing RNA regulatory stress in cancer cells through the accumulation of abnormal RNA.

The targets described by the company are (A) transcription (CDK12 inhibitor), (B) splicing (CLK inhibitor), (C) RNA degradation (undisclosed), and (D) tRNA recruitment (GCN2 inhibitor). Chordia also identifies a deeper understanding of cancers involving RNA regulatory abnormalities, the identification of new therapeutic targets, and personalized medicine strategies for delivering new drugs to patients likely to benefit as important research goals. The company says it actively pursues joint research with universities and other research institutions that have next-generation sequencing and advanced gene-editing technologies.

Official information: Research and pipeline (Japanese)

3. PRISM BioLab

PRISM BioLab says it is developing a drug discovery platform based on its proprietary PepMetics® technology. The company describes protein–protein interactions (PPIs) as important to intracellular signaling and molecular exchange, while noting that blocking disease-related protein interactions inside the cell with small molecules has been challenging.

According to PRISM BioLab, PepMetics® accurately reproduces helix structures involved in around 60% of protein-binding interfaces. By combining scaffolds that mimic multiple helix structures with amino-acid side chains, the technology can generate combinations on the order of hundreds of millions. The company says it aims to use this technology to create small molecules that block disease-related PPIs.

The company’s business model includes an in-house drug discovery business, in which it selects targets, develops clinical compounds, and licenses them. It also says it works with pharmaceutical companies, biotech companies, and researchers to advance the field. On July 2, 2026, the company posted an announcement that Eisai had published a paper on human pharmacokinetic analysis of E7386, which PRISM BioLab and Eisai jointly created.

Official information: PepMetics® technology · PRISM BioLab website · E7386 announcement (July 2, 2026)

4. FIMECS

FIMECS says it has built RaPPIDS™, a proprietary platform focused on targeted protein degradation inducers. Its hybrid business model combines an in-house pipeline with joint research projects with pharmaceutical companies. RaPPIDS™ is based on diversity-oriented synthesis (DOS) and consists of two stages: lead generation and lead optimization. The company continues to invest in research and development to improve and expand the platform.

According to FIMECS, during lead generation it uses probes combining multiple E3 ligase binders and linkers to create 20 to 50 target protein degradation inducers in a short period. This stage is used to identify a suitable E3 ligase and linker length. During lead optimization, the company uses a semi-automated, high-throughput DOS approach to synthesize and evaluate a broader range of compounds, fragmenting and recombining both linkers and E3 ligase binders.

FIMECS says it is currently difficult to accurately predict degradation activity by modeling the ternary complex of a target protein, a compound, and an E3 ligase. The company therefore considers it important to efficiently synthesize and evaluate many compounds. It has also developed a function for exploring new E3 ligase binders.

Official information: Research and business (Japanese)

5. RaQualia Pharma

RaQualia Pharma’s pipeline page lists programs for human and animal health and is dated August 14, 2026. The company says it updates the pipeline approximately quarterly.

One of the main human programs listed is tegoprazan (RQ-00000004), a potassium-competitive acid blocker (P-CAB) for gastroesophageal reflux disease. According to the page, tegoprazan is marketed in 20 countries, including South Korea, China, countries in Southeast Asia, and countries in Latin America. It is under review or being prepared for submission in markets including the United States and Vietnam, and Phase I has been completed in Japan. HK inno.N Corporation is listed as the licensee, with worldwide rights.

The page also lists RQ-00433412, a ghrelin receptor agonist for constipation and cancer-related anorexia/cachexia syndrome. Its preclinical studies are complete, and the company is conducting out-licensing activities worldwide. The TRPM8 antagonist RQ-00434739/XG2002 for chronic pain is in Phase I in Australia. Xgene Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd. is listed as the licensee for worldwide rights excluding Japan.

For animal health, the page lists the 5-HT4 agonist RQ-00000010 for intestinal motility disorders in dogs and cats. The program is in a proof-of-concept (POC) study, with Vetbiolix SAS listed as the licensee for worldwide rights. These development stages and territory details reflect the information on the company’s page as of August 14, 2026.

Official information: Pipeline (Japanese)

Gene Therapy and Genetic Medicines

6. Modalis Therapeutics

Modalis Therapeutics describes its proprietary epigenome-editing technology, CRISPR-GNDM®, as a way to control the ON/OFF switch of a target gene without cutting both strands of the DNA sequence. The company says this approach could be applied to develop treatments for a broad range of genetic diseases and may lead to safer therapeutic drugs. These are the company’s descriptions and expectations; they do not establish the safety or efficacy of any individual drug candidate.

The company’s pipeline page is dated August 2025 and lists programs owned and developed in-house. In muscle disorders, the programs include MDL-101 (LAMA2-related disease), MDL-202 (myotonic dystrophy type 1), MDL-201 (Duchenne muscular dystrophy), and MDL-103 (facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy). In cardiomyopathy, the page lists MDL-105 (dilated cardiomyopathy). In central nervous system disorders, it lists MDL-104 (tauopathy), MDL-206 (Angelman syndrome), and MDL-207 (Dravet syndrome). The page shows development categories from Discovery/Preclinical through Clinical. The text available for this review did not identify the individual stage of each program, so no stage is assigned here.

Official information: Pipeline (English) · Company overview (English)

Oncology and Immunotherapy

7. J-Pharma

J-Pharma describes its business as a single drug discovery and development segment centered on research and development. It aims to obtain approval from major regulatory authorities, including the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and conducts clinical development in appropriate locations and at appropriate times. The company says its global development structure brings together executives and R&D staff with international experience, CROs and consultants in Europe and the United States, and academic institutions. It lists Georgetown University in the United States and Turku PET Centre as major commissioned research and collaboration partners in multiple sclerosis.

According to the company’s business model page, its lead drug candidate, nanvuranlat, received Orphan Drug Designation from the U.S. FDA in April 2022, received IND clearance in September 2024, and entered a global Phase III clinical trial in December 2025. In a June 2026 announcement from J-Pharma and Uniphar Development, the companies reported that the first patient had been enrolled in the trial. J-Pharma describes cases of a global Phase III trial proceeding after FDA review based on domestic clinical data as rare.

For JPH034, a LAT1 inhibitor for multiple sclerosis, the company reported that the FDA’s IND safety review was completed in February 2026 and that the first participant was dosed in a U.S. Phase I clinical trial in March. The study is intended to evaluate safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics.

Official information: Business model (Japanese) · Nanvuranlat Phase III trial announcement (June 2026) · JPH034 Phase I trial announcement (March 2026, Japanese)

8. Noile-Immune Biotech

Noile-Immune Biotech says it is developing CAR-T cell therapies for solid tumors using its PRIME® technology. Its business page describes a model that combines in-house drug discovery with a “co-pipeline” model in which the company licenses its PRIME technology to other companies.

Official information: Business and PRIME® technology (English) · Pipeline

Cell Therapy and Regenerative Medicine

9. Heartseed

Heartseed identifies HS-001 as its lead pipeline program. According to the company, HS-001 is intended for heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), including dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM), old myocardial infarction (OMI), and dilated-phase hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (D-HCM). The product is allogeneic iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes, administered by intramyocardial injection. The company’s strategy is based on its view that transplanting a sufficient number of ventricular cardiomyocytes may offer the best approach to treating HFrEF.

For its next generation of research, Heartseed says it is studying cardiomyocytes made from autologous iPSCs and HLA-knockout iPSCs, as well as simpler administration methods that could be used at more medical institutions, in collaboration with other companies. A separate program, HS-005, is being studied in the domestic Phase I/II EMERALD trial using catheter-based administration; the company announced the first patient dose in June 2026. For HS-001, the company announced 52-week topline results from the domestic Phase I/II LAPiS trial in August 2026. HS-001 and HS-005 are separate programs.

Official information: Heartseed (English) · HS-005 Phase I/II trial announcement (English) · HS-001 52-week topline results announcement (English)

10. SanBio

SanBio received conditional and time-limited approval in Japan in July 2024 for AKUUGO® (vandefitemcel), a cell therapy for chronic motor paralysis following traumatic brain injury (TBI). In a CEO message dated April 22, 2026, the company described plans to prepare for consultations with the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) on expanding the indication to ischemic stroke, and with the U.S. FDA toward starting clinical trials in the United States.

AKUUGO® was listed for reimbursement on May 20, 2026, and launched on May 21. According to a summary of the company’s September 17, 2026 earnings Q&A, the first administration was planned for October, with six patients expected during the fiscal year. In the same Q&A, the company said it had agreed with the FDA on the design of a U.S. Phase III trial for chronic TBI, was finalizing the protocol, and aimed to start the trial in the next fiscal year. For chronic ischemic stroke, the company said it planned to consult with the PMDA, aiming to agree on a trial design in the next fiscal year. These are plans and projections reported at the time of the company’s announcements.

Official information: CEO message (April 22, 2026) · AKUUGO® launch announcement (Japanese) · Earnings Q&A (September 17, 2026, Japanese)

11. Healios

Healios lists regenerative medicine using iPSCs and somatic stem cells among its areas of research and development. The company identifies age-related macular degeneration and liver disease in its iPSC programs, and ischemic stroke and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) in its somatic stem cell programs.

In the detailed pipeline table in its business plan dated March 23, 2026, Healios lists the HLCM051 ARDS program as being in preparation for a Japanese marketing application, with the global Phase III trial started and a U.S. IND in preparation. For the acute ischemic stroke program, the table says the development and application strategy is under review.

Official information: Research and development status (English) · Business plan and growth potential (March 2026, Japanese)

What to Consider When Evaluating Biotech Companies in Japan

A company’s technical focus is one starting point when building a shortlist. Before reaching out, consider the following in light of your objectives in Japan:

  1. Define the purpose of the collaboration. Are you looking for an out-licensing partner, an in-licensing opportunity, co-development, research collaboration, or a technology provider?
  2. Be specific about the scientific area of interest. Clarify the target, modality, indication, and data supporting your proposal.
  3. Review development status and territorial rights. Look into the asset’s status, existing partners, and rights already licensed in Japan or other regions.
  4. Identify the right team and contact route. Business development discussions and scientific research collaborations may involve different materials and stakeholders.
  5. Suggest a practical next step. A focused technical meeting or evaluation project can be more useful than a broad proposal to “explore synergies.”

These steps can help narrow a long company list to candidates that fit your program and the decisions you need to make.
