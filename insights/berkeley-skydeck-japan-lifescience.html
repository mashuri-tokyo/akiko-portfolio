Japan Biotech · Berkeley SkyDeck

Japanese Life Science Startups
at Berkeley SkyDeck

Two different pathways—Accelerator Cohort and IPP—have connected Japanese founders with Berkeley’s startup ecosystem.

Akiko Yoshida, MSc · Published September 27, 2026

Japan and the United States have long-standing ties in biotechnology and life sciences. One place where those connections become visible is Berkeley SkyDeck, UC Berkeley’s startup accelerator. Since fiscal year 2021, JETRO has worked with SkyDeck through programs supporting Japanese startups’ international expansion.

I first became interested in the Japanese companies taking part in these programs because I had worked at Genomelink, which once had an office at SkyDeck. At the time, I was focused on finding genetic traits that could make genomics engaging for consumers: reviewing research, checking the strength of the evidence, and following new developments in genomics. I traveled between Tokyo and Berkeley, and that period remains meaningful to me.

Looking into SkyDeck again, I found that “participating in SkyDeck” can describe different routes. The Accelerator Cohort is SkyDeck’s core accelerator, with investment from the Berkeley SkyDeck Fund for selected teams. The Innovation Partners Program (IPP) brings startups to SkyDeck through partner organizations. The programs offer different structures, so I’ve kept the participation categories separate below.

Program How startups participate What the program offers
Accelerator Cohort Startups selected into SkyDeck’s core accelerator SkyDeck Fund investment for selected teams, mentorship, and accelerator programming
IPP Startups participate through partner organizations Workshops, advisor office hours, networking, and a showcase

Accelerator Cohort: Genomelink and ThinkCyte

Cohort · Batch 6

Genomelink

A consumer-facing “DNA App Store”

Program — Berkeley SkyDeck Accelerator Cohort, Batch 6

Focus — Consumer genomics, genetic traits, DNA data

Founder interview — SkyDeck interview with Tomohiro “Tomo” Takano

Genomelink envisioned a “DNA App Store” where people could upload their genetic data and access a range of analyses and apps. Berkeley SkyDeck’s company portfolio lists Genomelink as Batch 6.

In a SkyDeck interview, co-founder Tomohiro “Tomo” Takano described how uncertain the company’s early U.S. journey was. The team had struggled to gain traction with its first product, had limited funds, and was considering returning to Japan when SkyDeck’s acceptance came through.

Takano said the company began its Series A fundraising process around SkyDeck’s alumni Demo Day, where it built connections with investors. Genomelink later joined Y Combinator, used YC’s Demo Day to continue building investor relationships, and ultimately raised a $10 million Series A.

I remember the work happening alongside those milestones: searching for traits people might find surprising, checking what the research actually supported, and thinking about how to turn genomics into a useful consumer experience.

Cohort · Batch 7

ThinkCyte

AI-powered, label-free cell analysis and sorting

Program — Berkeley SkyDeck Accelerator Cohort, Batch 7

Focus — Ghost Cytometry, cell morphology, AI-enabled sorting

Program history — ThinkCyte’s graduation announcement

ThinkCyte’s Ghost Cytometry technology captures cell morphology as waveform data when cells pass through structured illumination, then uses AI to classify and sort cells. The company completed SkyDeck’s Cohort Program in 2018.

In its announcement, ThinkCyte described a six-month program that included a $100,000 investment and mentoring on product strategy, fundraising plans, and team building. SkyDeck’s portfolio lists ThinkCyte as Batch 7.

Genomelink brought a consumer genomics concept to the program; ThinkCyte brought a new approach to cell analysis and sorting. Their technologies and customers differ, but both show Japanese-founded teams building companies across the U.S.–Japan boundary.

Japanese life science companies in SkyDeck’s IPP

SkyDeck’s Innovation Partners Program works with partner organizations that send startups to SkyDeck. Participants can access workshops, advisor office hours, networking, and a showcase. In Japan, examples of partner routes include JETRO and university-affiliated venture organizations.

Field Note

Beyond the acceptance announcement

Joining an accelerator is one milestone in a company’s history. What I’m most interested in is what happens next: whom the founders meet, which research or business questions they work through, and how those conversations shape their next steps.

Looking back into Genomelink’s SkyDeck years also brought back my own experience: moving between Tokyo and Berkeley, reading papers for promising science, and thinking about how to make it meaningful to users.

I’d like to keep following these companies beyond their program names—to understand whose problems their technologies aim to solve, and how they build the partnerships and evidence needed to reach those users.

What problem can this technology actually solve—and for whom?

Assessing Japan for your life science company?

For U.S. life science companies assessing Japan, understanding the ecosystem starts with more than a company list. It means learning which local needs a technology could address, who might use it, and which research or commercial partners could help evaluate the opportunity.
